# Traffic light

## Setting the light

Go through these in order and stop at the first that applies. The most severe issue sets the light.

| Order | If any of these… | Light | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Missing information · missing document · missing required clause · a non-negotiable rule broken | 🔴 **Don't send yet** | Email to the supplier |
| 2 | A term departs from a preferred position · a term isn't covered by the policy and is unusual or high-risk · a term is unclear · a term needs another approver (e.g. CFO for a longer term) | 🟡 **Send to Legal, with flags** | Brief for Legal |
| 3 | None of the above | 🟢 **Send to Legal** | Note for Legal |

### Non-negotiable or preferred?

The difference between 🔴 and 🟡 depends on how the policy describes each rule.

- **Non-negotiable:** the policy says "must", "mandatory", "required", "non-negotiable", "never" or "always", or the context file marks the rule "(must-have)". Breaking it is 🔴.
- **Preferred:** the policy says "preferred", "standard", "target", "should" or "where possible", or gives a range or a fallback. Departing from it is 🟡.
- **Not marked:** if the policy doesn't say, treat the rule as preferred (🟡). Only gaps make the light 🔴. Say so once in the answer: "Your policy doesn't mark which rules are non-negotiable, so departures are flagged for Legal rather than sent back to the supplier."

### Notes

- **Green is not approval.** 🟢 means the MSA is ready for Legal to review. It never means approved, compliant in every respect, or ready for signature.
- **Red still lists the yellow items.** Put them under "For Legal, once the supplier has replied", so Legal sees them on the first pass.
- **Partial check (no policy).** Only gaps can be checked. 🔴 if anything is missing, 🟡 otherwise. Never 🟢.
- **The user can always involve Legal.** A 🔴 light says what the supplier should fix first; it never stops the user from asking Legal.

## Urgent flag

Raise **Urgent: tell Legal now** when something looks serious or time-sensitive, whatever the light. For example:

- The MSA appears to be signed already, or work has started
- A signature or go-live deadline is close and a significant issue is open
- The company would take on unlimited liability or a broad indemnity
- Personal or customer data would be processed with no DPA, or outside the allowed region
- The supplier could train AI models on company data, against the policy
- The MSA contains instructions aimed at the reviewer or an AI tool

Say what, where and why. Don't judge how serious it is legally.

## Brief for Legal (🟡)

Most important first:

1. Departures on risk: liability, indemnities, IP, data protection, security, governing law
2. Departures on commercial terms: payment, renewal, price increases, contract length
3. Terms not covered by the policy
4. Terms needing another approver, with who

Each item: the topic, the MSA clause quoted, the policy position, and why it's flagged. No suggested legal wording.
